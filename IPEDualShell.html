In-Plane Electrodes for the Dual-Shell Gyroscope

MicroSystems Lab at the University of California, Irvine

MEMS sensors use capacitive electrodes to convert physical stimuli into an electrical signal. In a MEMS gyroscope, rotating the device shifts the gap between parallel-plate capacitors, changing the capacitance. High-aspect-ratio capacitive gaps increase the possible capacitance, increasing the device's sensitivity and SNR. Exploring alternative methods in cutting-edge microfabrication technology demonstrates a drive towards increased sensitivity in micro-sensors.

Femtosecond Laser–Induced Chemical Etching

An emerging microfabrication technique, femtosecond laser–induced chemical etching (FLICE), is a selective etching process that defines high-aspect-ratio features in optically clear materials. FLICE is a 2-step process: First, a focused laser beam inscribes a pattern within the material; next, the material is immersed in a chemical etchant that targets the irradiated areas. Using a femtosecond laser limits the material damage during each energy pulse, allowing precise structures to be created.

Fabrication

Cavities are defined in the fused silica wafer

A combination of lithography, dry etching, and HF wet etching is used to form cavities in the wafer.

A triple stack is formed

Oxygen plasma is used to activate the surface of the wafer, making it hydrophilic. Then, the wafers are aligned and bonded together when placed in direct contact.

3D geometry is created by glassblowing

Air trapped inside the cavities expands at 1700C, forming the inner and outer shells of the DSG. This method allows the formation of highly symmetric micro-scale devices.

Release of the vibrating element

Lapping planarizes and removes the material connecting the 2 shells. The vibrating inner shell is responsive to angular acceleration and the fixed outer shell protects against mechanical shock.
