Master's Project

INRF Graduate Research Fellow

As a graduate fellow at the Integrated Nanosystems Research Facility (INRF), I gained access to a class 1,000/10,000 cleanroom and performed equipment testing and validation. I gathered experimental data to ensure consistent tool operation and repeatable processes. Also, I wrote standard operating procedures for each of the tools listed below.

An oxygen plasma process produces a blue glow seen through the viewport of the Oxford system.

Reactive Ion Etching - Oxford PlasmaPro 80 RIE

The Oxford system is a compact system with a graphite platform that accommodates wafers up to 200 mm in diameter. Strict contamination control is required to produce uniform etching rates across the wafer. I ran experimental tests to develop a chamber cleaning and seasoning process for the CF4 etch of SiO2 that achieved etch rate uniformities within 5% across a 100 mm wafer. All uniformities were calculated following ASTM F1618-96 [1].

The shape and density of a vapor cloud directly affects the thin-film uniformity, with n=1 indicating an ideal, wide spread of vapor.

Thermal Evaporation - Angstrom EvoVac

The Angstrom system at INRF deposits materials by resistive heating or electron beam evaporation. This combined system also supports ion beam milling. A uniform thickness across deposited films ensures consistent mechanical and thermal properties in fabricated devices. I conducted analytical film uniformity analysis based on [2] and experimentally validated QCM sensor measurements to support precise control of deposited film thickness.

The MicroWriter ML3 tool.

Maskless Lithography - Micro Writer ML3 Pro

The Micro Writer is a direct-write lithography system for dynamic manufacturing environments. Exposure patterns are directly transformed from graphic software (e.g., .cif files from CleWin and L-edit) into projected UV light.
